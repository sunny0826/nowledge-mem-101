## Your turn

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose one topic that will keep changing">
    Pick a long-running project, customer, research area, or operating practice with at least five related memories. The example topic is the Atlas annual planning process.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Browse the topic in the Library">
    Open **Library → Wiki** and choose the closest topic. Read its summary, open one entity, then follow one related entity. If the Wiki is still empty, open **Graph** and run **Wiki Topic Detection**, or use memory search for this first audit.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name what is present and missing">
    Identify one current decision, the strongest supporting item, and one question your existing knowledge cannot answer.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the audit in Timeline">
    Keep it short enough to revise later:

    ```text
    Atlas planning knowledge audit

    Scope: How Atlas runs and evaluates its annual planning review.
    Current decision: Pilot one review in September.
    Strongest evidence: Quarterly review feedback shows cross-team dependencies surface too late.
    First gap: We have not defined how the pilot will be judged.
    ```
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What you should see

<Check>
  Timeline contains one readable audit that gives the topic a boundary and identifies the next missing piece.
</Check>

## Why this matters

A useful boundary keeps the system focused on a recurring question. It gives new memories and sources a reason to belong without forcing you to design a complete hierarchy first.
