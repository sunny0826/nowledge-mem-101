Nowledge Mem keeps source material and durable takeaways as different things. Add one source intact, then decide which single insight deserves to join the long-term graph.

## What you'll accomplish

<Check>
  You'll keep one searchable source in the Library and save one verified, source-backed memory from it.

## Your turn

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add the source">
    Open **Library** and import a report, retrospective, interview transcript, or plan you are allowed to use. Wait until its status is **Searchable**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Study it against the topic">
    Open the source and choose **Study with AI**. Ask one bounded question:

    ```text
    Which claim in this source supports, challenges, or changes the Atlas annual planning memories? Show the relevant source passage before proposing anything to save.
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the proposed takeaway">
    Compare the proposal with the cited passage. Make it self-contained, preserve any uncertainty, and remove claims the source does not support.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save one useful memory">
    Save the strongest verified proposal. Leave the rest in the source unless each item will be useful independently later.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What you should see

<Check>
  The Library still contains the complete source, while Memories contains one concise takeaway that can be found and understood on its own.
</Check>

<Note>
**Study with AI** proposes knowledge. Nothing is written to long-term memory until you choose what to save.
</Note>

## Why this matters

The source preserves provenance and detail. The memory preserves the durable takeaway. Keeping both avoids turning a large document into either an opaque attachment or dozens of low-value fragments.
