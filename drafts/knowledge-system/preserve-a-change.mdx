## Your turn

<Steps>
  <Step title="Find an older decision">
    Open the decision at the center of your topic. Confirm that it records what was believed or chosen at that time.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the new state separately">
    In Timeline, capture what changed, why, and what remains unresolved. Do not edit the old decision into the new one.

    ```text
    Atlas annual planning update — October 2026

    After the September pilot, Atlas will replace the single annual review with two focused planning reviews each year. The pilot surfaced useful dependencies, but one large review created too much preparation work. The team still needs to choose the two review months.
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Inspect the relationship">
    After Background Intelligence processes the new memory, open its detail view and look for an EVOLVES relationship such as `replaces`, `enriches`, `confirms`, or `challenges`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review rather than assume">
    If Mem surfaces a challenge, compare both memories. Keep the history that reflects what was known at each point instead of assuming the newest text is automatically correct.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What you should see

<Check>
  Both the earlier decision and the update remain readable. When evolution processing finishes, their relationship appears with the history instead of silently overwriting it.
</Check>

<Note>
Knowledge-evolution detection requires Background Intelligence and may finish after this lesson. The durable success state is that you kept the old and new knowledge as separate, dated memories.
</Note>

## Why this matters

A long-term system should answer both “what is current?” and “how did we get here?” Preserving the earlier state makes later decisions auditable.
