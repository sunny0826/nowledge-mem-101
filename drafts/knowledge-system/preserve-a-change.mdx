## Before you start

Choose a decision that has actually changed, and have the earlier record ready. For practice, continue the fictional Atlas timeline from the September decision to the October update below. Use your real dates and evidence for real work.

## Your turn

<Steps>
  <Step title="Find an older decision">
    Open the annual review decision from lesson 2. In the practice case, it approved one review per year starting with the September pilot. Record its name and date; distinguish that decision from a mere suggestion.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the new state separately">
    Paste a separate update into **Timeline** and press Enter. State the earlier decision, the changed choice, the evidence, and the unresolved work:

    ```text
    Atlas annual planning update — fictional practice, 2026-10-01

    Earlier decision: Atlas annual review decision, 2026-09-01 — one review each year.
    New decision: replace that annual cadence with two smaller planning reviews each year.
    Reason: in the September pilot, the team found useful dependencies but reported too much preparation for one large review.
    Unchanged: reuse existing quarterly notes and track preparation time.
    Open: choose the two review months and agree the preparation limit.
    ```

    Keep the earlier record intact. Correcting a typo is an edit; a changed decision needs this dated explanation of what came before it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the two records together">
    Open both memories and compare the old cadence, new cadence, reason, and open questions. Then ask in **Timeline**:

    ```text
    According to the Atlas annual planning update dated 2026-10-01, what changed from the 2026-09-01 decision, and what stayed the same? Distinguish the approved change from the remaining open questions.
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Inspect evolution if it has been detected">
    If Background Intelligence is enabled, open the memory details and inspect any detected EVOLVES relationship. A `replaces` link represents a superseding decision; `enriches` adds detail. `confirms` and `challenges` supply evidence and do not by themselves replace the original.

    Check that the detected meaning matches your records. A challenge needs your judgment; a newer date alone does not decide which claim is correct.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What you should see

<Check>
  Both dated records remain readable. The answer identifies the move from one annual review to two smaller reviews, preserves the reuse constraint, and keeps the review months and preparation limit open.
</Check>

<Note>
EVOLVES detection runs through Background Intelligence and may not produce a link during this lesson. The two checked records are enough to complete the exercise. When an old memory is marked superseded, use its history to inspect it; everyday search may favor the current version.
</Note>

## Why this matters

A long-term system should answer both “what is current?” and “how did we get here?” Preserving the earlier state makes later decisions auditable.
