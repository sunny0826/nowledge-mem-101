Nowledge Mem works with durable units, not one ever-growing note. Curate a small core whose items can each be understood without the conversation that created them.

## What you'll accomplish

<Check>
  You'll have three self-contained memories with useful types, a shared label, and appropriate importance.
</Check>

## Your turn

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose three different jobs">
    Find or create one decision, one learning, and one procedure for your topic. For Atlas, these could cover the pilot decision, what earlier reviews revealed, and how the review will be prepared.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make each memory stand alone">
    Open each item in **Memories**. Rewrite vague references such as “we agreed” or “this process” so a future reader can understand the subject, outcome, and reason.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the type and importance">
    Confirm **Type** matches the job of each memory. Give the central decision higher **Importance** than background context; do not mark every item as critical.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply one shared label">
    Add a focused label such as `atlas-planning` to all three memories. Reuse an existing label when it already means the same thing.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What you should see

<Check>
  Memory search for `atlas-planning` returns three distinct units: what was decided, what was learned, and what to do.
</Check>

<Note>
If a newer decision changes an older one, do not rewrite history here. Lesson 5 shows how to preserve the change as a new memory.
</Note>

## Why this matters

Types tell an AI how to use a memory. Labels provide a broad human-readable bucket. Importance helps the most consequential knowledge surface before background detail.
