## Before you start

Have the annual review decision from lesson 2 and the source-backed takeaway from lesson 3 ready in the same Space. Read both and check whether a suitable link already exists.

## Your turn

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose a meaningful pair">
    Write the intended relationship as a sentence: "The dependency observation supports trying an earlier cross-team review." The observation is the evidence; the decision is what it supports. The source does not prove that an annual cadence is best.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open them in the graph">
    Open **Graph**, select the source-backed memory, choose **Connect memories**, then select the annual review decision. Use Memory nodes, rather than the source document or an entity node.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name the relationship">
    Enter `supports` and add a reason. Read the pair in that order before saving:

    ```text
    The retrospective documents a dependency found after plans were committed. It supports testing earlier cross-team coordination, but does not establish that an annual cadence is best.
    ```

    Save the connection. If the evidence conflicts with a decision, `contradicts` may be a better relation; choose the meaning supported by your records.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Inspect the saved edge">
    Select the line between the memories and read its relation and reason. Confirm it connects the intended pair and expresses evidence supporting the trial, not the decision proving the evidence.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What you should see

<Check>
  The graph shows a line between the two memories, and the edge explains why they belong together.
</Check>

<Tip>
Use labels for a broad category. Use a Memory Link for a specific relationship. A newer version of a decision belongs in its EVOLVES history, covered in lesson 5; typing `replaces` as an ordinary link name does not establish that history.
</Tip>

## Why this matters

A saved relationship makes your interpretation inspectable. Future readers and connected agents can follow the reason and decide how much weight the underlying evidence deserves.
