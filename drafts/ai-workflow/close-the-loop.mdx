## Before you start

Have the brief, lesson 3 checkpoint, and lesson 4 draft ready. Decide which parts you accept and which still need review.

## Your turn

<Steps>
  <Step title="Separate the durable result from the draft">
    Keep the accepted decision, the draft or its accessible location, unresolved evidence, and the next action. Check every "needs evidence" marker before calling anything ready to publish.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save a dated update in Timeline">
    Adapt this fictional example to your actual result and date. Paste it into **Timeline** and press Enter:

    ```text
    Atlas landing page update — 2026-09-12

    Updates: Atlas landing page handoff checkpoint, whose next action was to draft the hero and section four.
    Accepted: keep "Try Atlas" as the single call to action.
    Completed: the hero and section four drafts are written; this drafting step is no longer the next action.
    Status: ready for review, not approved for publication. Section four still has a "needs evidence" marker.
    Next step: ask the product owner for a verified example, then revise and review section four before publishing.
    ```

    Include the accepted copy below the record, or add its actual file name or document link and confirm that the next tool can open it. Keep the earlier checkpoint as the record of the previous stage.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Test the restart point">
    Confirm the update appears in **Timeline**. In a fresh session with a connected AI, ask:

    ```text
    Search Nowledge Mem for the latest Atlas landing page update and its earlier handoff checkpoint. What is complete, what still blocks publication, where is the draft, and what is the next action? Identify the records you used.
    ```

    If it repeats the old drafting action, ask it to compare the dated update with the earlier checkpoint. If the update is unclear, clarify the status in that memory and repeat the check.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What you should see

<Check>
  The AI finds the draft and says that evidence review comes next. It does not restart completed drafting or describe the page as ready to publish.
</Check>

## Why this matters

Closing the loop keeps useful context current. Each pass leaves a clearer starting point for the next one, whether you return tomorrow, switch tools, or hand the work to someone else.
