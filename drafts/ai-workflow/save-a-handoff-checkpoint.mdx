## Your turn

<Steps>
  <Step title="Review the first AI's output">
    Identify one settled choice, one unfinished item, and the exact next action. Do not save every message from the session as the handoff.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the checkpoint in Timeline">
    Adapt this format to your work:

    ```text
    Atlas landing page handoff checkpoint

    Completed: drafted a six-section outline.
    Decision: open with the cost of status chasing, then introduce the weekly planning flow.
    Why: it matches the operations-lead audience and the existing brief.
    Still open: choose the proof point for section four.
    Next step: write a first version of the hero and section four copy.
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep the original conversation when you need it">
    If your tool supports saving a full session, keep it as a thread for detailed history. The checkpoint remains the quick, durable way to resume the task.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What you should see

<Check>
  You can read the checkpoint on its own and know what was completed, why it was chosen, what remains open, and what to do next.
</Check>

<Note>
Different connectors handle conversation capture differently. A handoff checkpoint works even when full-session capture is unavailable.
</Note>

## Why this matters

The next AI should not have to infer your task state from a long conversation. A checkpoint makes the transition explicit while keeping the important reasoning visible.
