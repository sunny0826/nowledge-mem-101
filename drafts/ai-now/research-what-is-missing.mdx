## Before you start

Keep the lesson 3 evidence brief and one open question ready. The time estimate covers setting up and reviewing the request; a research run may take longer.

<Note>
Use a general question for public research. Keep confidential details and personal data out of web search requests.
</Note>

## Your turn

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose one open question">
    Decide what kind of evidence could answer it. Team capacity needs an internal estimate or trial; external articles cannot establish it. For that route, record who could answer and what to measure, then continue to lesson 5.

    For the public-research route, ask whether longer-horizon planning reviews have evidence of reducing missed cross-team dependencies.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enable Deep Research for a public question">
    In the AI Now conversation, turn on **Research**. If it is unavailable, keep the gap unresolved and record the public evidence you still need; do not substitute an uncited answer.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ask for sources, not a generic verdict">
    Keep the research scoped:

    ```text
    Find public evidence on whether longer-horizon cross-team planning reviews reduce missed dependencies compared with quarterly team reviews alone. Prefer original studies, documented trials, or first-hand case reports.

    For each finding, give its source link, publication date, the actual review cadence studied, and what was measured. Separate measured outcomes from advice or opinion. If you find no direct evidence for this comparison, say so. Do not treat our fictional Atlas interviews as public evidence.
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Compare the research with your own evidence">
    Open the most relevant cited source. Check that it contains the claimed result and applies to the same kind of review. A weekly retrospective or a vendor's recommendation may be useful background, but does not directly answer this comparison.

    In the conversation, record the finding, its source and date, the limitation, and what remains unknown. If no direct evidence was found, keep that result and plan a small internal test.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What you should see

<Check>
  You have either a verified public finding with its limits, or an explicit gap with an internal follow-up. The absence of direct evidence has not become a claim that the idea works or fails.
</Check>

## Why this matters

Research is most useful after you know what is missing. Your own memories and source material set the question; current public sources help fill a specific gap.
