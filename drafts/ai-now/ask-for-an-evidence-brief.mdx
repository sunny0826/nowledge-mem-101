## Your turn

<Steps>
  <Step title="Set the output shape">
    Ask for a brief that makes its reasoning reviewable:

    ```text
    Based only on the Atlas memories and customer interview notes in this task, write a decision brief about an annual planning review.

    Include:
    1. Evidence that supports it
    2. Evidence that challenges it
    3. Open questions
    4. Assumptions you had to make
    5. The smallest next step that would reduce uncertainty
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the evidence before the recommendation">
    Check that each finding points back to a memory or source passage. Ask the AI to correct any claim that lacks supporting material.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep open questions open">
    Do not turn a missing fact into a conclusion. Mark it for research in the next lesson.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What you should see

<Check>
  The brief shows what your evidence supports, what it challenges, and which questions still need an answer.
</Check>

## Why this matters

An evidence brief is more useful than a polished answer with hidden assumptions. It gives you a clear boundary between the knowledge you own and the information you still need.
