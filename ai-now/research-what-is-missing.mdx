The evidence brief in Nowledge Mem names what is missing. Choose a way to investigate one gap: your own team for internal facts, or AI Now Deep Research for public evidence.

## What you'll accomplish

<Check>
  You'll record a checked finding, or a clearly unresolved question, with the next step needed to answer it.
</Check>

## Learn with the simulation

On a desktop browser, go to [Your turn](#your-turn) and click **Start the simulation**. Follow the popup hints to send a request, read its reply, and check the result. Each lesson prepares its fictional Atlas materials; no model setup is needed.

<Note>
The simulation runs locally with preset replies. It does not call AI or search the web, and refreshing resets the practice. On mobile, read the steps first and use a desktop browser for the interactive exercise.
</Note>

## Before practising in the real app

Keep the lesson 3 evidence brief and one open question ready. The time estimate covers setting up and reviewing the request; a research run may take longer.

<Note>
Use a general question for public research. Keep confidential details and personal data out of web search requests.
</Note>

## What you should see

<Check>
  You have an explicit gap and a next action. If you ran Deep Research, you also have a finding checked against its source, or a record that this run found no relevant evidence. Neither outcome proves that the review works or fails.
</Check>

<Note>
Planning an internal follow-up completes the investigation-planning route. The Deep Research exercise is complete only after a real run and source review, or a recorded no-evidence result. If sources could not be checked, keep that verification pending.
</Note>

## Why this matters

Research is most useful after you know what is missing. Your own memories and source material set the question; current public sources help fill a specific gap.
