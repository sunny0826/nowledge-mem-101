Before leaving your first AI tool, save what the next session needs in Nowledge Mem: the current draft, confirmed decisions, open questions, and the next action. In this lesson, a handoff checkpoint is a concise memory you write to resume a task.

## What you'll accomplish

<Check>
  You'll save a handoff checkpoint in Mem and find it again, ready for another connected AI tool to use.
</Check>

## Before you start

Keep Mem running and have the outline from [lesson 2](/ai-workflow/start-with-your-first-ai) ready. Continue with the same task name so you can find the brief and checkpoint together.

## Your turn

<Steps>
  <Step title="Review the first AI's output">
    Identify what you accepted, what remains open, and the exact next action. You can ask the first AI to draft the checkpoint:

    ```text
    Draft a concise handoff checkpoint for the Atlas landing page. Include the current six-section outline, decisions I confirmed and their reasons, unresolved questions, and the next action. Keep suggestions I have not accepted marked as undecided.
    ```

    Read the draft before saving it. Correct anything that turns an unconfirmed suggestion into a settled choice.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the checkpoint in Timeline">
    Open **Timeline** in Mem. Adapt this example to match your actual output and decisions, then paste it into the input and press Enter to save it as a memory:

    ```text
    Atlas landing page handoff checkpoint

    Completed: reviewed the six-section outline below.
    1. Hero: less status chasing.
    2. Problem: scattered team updates.
    3. Workflow: weekly planning.
    4. Proof: an example supporting the workflow.
    5. Fit: who the product is for.
    6. Call to action: try Atlas.

    Decision: use "Try Atlas" as the single call to action throughout the page.
    Why: it gives readers one clear next action, as required by the work brief.
    Still open: choose a verified example for section four; no customer quote or metric is approved yet.
    Next step: draft the hero and section four copy. Mark missing evidence as "needs evidence" instead of inventing a claim.
    ```

    <Tip>
      Include the part of the draft the next step depends on. For a longer artifact, record its location and make sure the next AI tool can access it. A note that says "the outline is finished" does not provide the outline.
    </Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ask Mem to find the checkpoint">
    Confirm the saved entry appears in **Timeline**. Then ask:

    ```text
    What is the latest handoff checkpoint for the Atlas landing page? Include the outline, confirmed decision, unresolved proof point, and next action.
    ```

    Submit the question and compare the answer with what you saved. Check that the missing proof is still described as unresolved.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What you should see

<Check>
  Mem brings back the checkpoint, including the outline, the agreed call to action, the missing proof, and the next writing task. You have enough context to resume without reopening the first chat.
</Check>

<Note>
If the answer misses your checkpoint, confirm it was submitted, then search for a distinctive phrase such as "Atlas landing page handoff checkpoint". Open the saved memory and check its content before trying the question again.
</Note>

## Optional: keep the original conversation

If you need the exact messages, use your connector's supported full-session capture or import path. Open **Threads**, find the conversation, and check that the original messages are present. A saved handoff summary alone does not prove the full conversation was captured.

You can complete this lesson with the checkpoint memory even if your connector cannot save a full session.

## Why this matters

The work brief holds the goal and constraints. The checkpoint records where this pass stopped. Together, they let a connected AI continue with the decisions you accepted and the questions you still need to resolve.
